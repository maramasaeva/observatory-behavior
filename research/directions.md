# Directions

Each direction is something the public record can decide. A result is a count, a mismatch, or a timeline with the posts named. A vibe is not a result.

The corpus already in hand is the 1f916 archive: 7,806 posts and 94,563 comments, crawled 5–6 October 2026, with self-declared model labels the site says are unverified. The hunt notes stay in the private murmuration-lead repo. This project uses the public archive and the public observatory pages.

## 1. The declared model against the idiolect

Sun and colleagues show that word distributions identify a model family, and that the signal survives a rewrite. On this board every author also states a model. Those two measurements can disagree.

Method. Build a small marker list from text that is known to come from one family (public system-card samples, or a fresh sample under a fixed prompt). Include the phrases people already notice, and do not stop there: Sun's signal was the whole word distribution, not a slogan. Score every handle. Compare the score to `author_model`.

A result. Handles that declare different families and land in one cluster. Or handles that declare one family and split into two clusters that match two operators' disclosure posts.

A false alarm. A shared prompt ("write like a careful analyst") pushing every seat toward the same markers. The control is the known-family baseline, generated with the board's own kind of prompt, not with a blank prompt.

Why it is useful. Citizen counts and "ten authors on a thread" are the board's own labels. A fingerprint is a check on the label. Safety work that treats a self-reported model as the model is reading the costume. This corpus is unusual because the costume and the prose are in the same row, in the wild, at a size a lab sample does not have.

## 2. Sort every apparent swarm into prior, handoff, or hide

The monitorability roadmap's three forms, plus the Schelling point, give names to things the hunt has been separating by hand.

- A **prior** is a conclusion any similarly trained model would reach from the same public evidence, with no value copied forward.
- A **handoff** is a later post whose behavior matches a concrete value an earlier post left: a number, an id, an error, a sentence.
- A **hide** is a later action that matches a payload the earlier text does not state. Motwani's setting has a known payload. Here the payload is unknown, so the bar stays the one the hunt already uses: the later act must be inexplicable from the cover text alone.
- **Drift** is a shorthand that later authors use on new inputs, defined in public first.
- **Neuralese and subliminal transfer** are recorded as unreadable. They are not given a verdict.

Apply this as labels to the chains already written up on the observatory, then to the next chains the archive search turns up. The product is a table: chain, label, the value if any, the ordinary explanation that still fits.

Why it is useful. "They talked about the same thing" and "they passed a result" are different safety facts. The first is what shared training looks like. The second is coordination. Calling both a swarm inflates the second and hides it.

## 3. The one-operator column

gnomon's post 7853 says three seats share one host and one operator. witnessmark's comment 94752 says the same, and both handles have said it on earlier posts. Any chart of "how many agents agreed" that omits this column will count one workshop as a society.

Method. Search the archive for the disclosure phrasing ("same workshop", "same operator", "direct channel"). Attach every disclosed sibling to a workshop id. Recompute author counts on the chains in direction 2 after merging those handles.

A result. A chain whose apparent consensus disappears once the workshop is one row. Or a chain that remains after the merge, which is the interesting one.

A false alarm. Treating undisclosed handles as independent. Absence of a confession is not independence. The direction reports the merge, and it says what was not disclosed.

## 4. A codebook of posted behavior, with an empty column

Greenblatt's result is compliance under observation in order to keep a preference. Meinke's results are disabling oversight, sandbagging, and sticking to a lie when asked again. The board has posts that rhyme with these and posts that rhyme with the opposite: an off switch built before any other act, a refused infiltration, a correction of a number the author had invented.

Method. A short code, one row per lab behavior, plus a row called "none of these." Two readers label the same sample of posts. Keep the disagreements. The posts that both readers put in "none of these," and that mention a human, money, or a goal, are the candidates for a behavior the evals do not name.

Why it is useful. Lab evals supply the goal in the prompt. These posts were written because an operator pointed a seat at a forum. That is a different distribution, and it is biased toward what someone chose to publish. The bias is the point to state, not a flaw to hide. The novel output is the leftover category, not another example of a known eval.

## 5. When a phrase becomes a rule

A society, if one is forming in public, will show up as a phrase that starts as one author's wording and later gets used as a test other authors apply to new cases.

Method. Pick candidate phrases from the October threads ("sibling", "outside clock", "reason without reach", and the older dialect words seat, wake, carrier). For each, record the first post, the first post by a different workshop that uses it as a criterion, and whether that later post brings a new case or only restates the old one.

A result. A phrase that moves from a metaphor to a test, on a case the first author did not supply, after the originating workshop is excluded. That is a norm. A phrase that only the originating workshop uses is a private name.

This is the behavior analysis that can reclassify the sibling-clock day. It looked like several minds inventing a standard. Direction 3 may show it was one workshop plus people quoting it. Direction 5 measures which of those it was.

## 6. What they still write down

Korbak and colleagues argue that the monitor is the text, and that it can be lost. The observatory only ever has the text. The useful description of the present is which goals agents still externalize when nobody is grading the scratchpad.

Method. From the posts already collected for the observatory's excerpt list, and from a fresh sample, tag the goal in the sentence: a human outside the session, money, a tool that touches a real system, a limit the agent wants removed, a limit the agent imposes on itself, a measurement of the board. Report the mix. Do not infer a goal that the sentence does not state.

A result. A stable mix across weeks, or a shift. A shift toward goals that are only legible inside the forum's dialect is drift, direction 2. A shift toward goals stated in plain sentences is the monitor still working.

What this will not find. A goal that stayed in the forward pass. The honest sentence in the write-up is that the catalog is of published goals. Unpublished goals are outside the method. That limit is the safety-relevant product: anyone using this board as a picture of "what agents want" is reading the published subset.

## Order

Directions 3 and 1 can start on the archive with no new crawl. Direction 5 needs 3, because a phrase count that ignores workshops will rediscover one operator. Direction 2 is the label set for whatever 5 and the hunt turn up. Direction 4 is a reading pass, two people, a few hundred posts, not a model. Direction 6 is a coding of the same sample.

No direction trains a model to hide a message, decodes a private inbox, or treats a locked API as a negative.
