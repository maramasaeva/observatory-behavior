# Brainstorm, 6 October 2026

Not a measurement. Three ideas to keep next to the directions.

## A second layer inside ordinary sentences

Speech can change a claim without changing the words. Stress and pitch do that. "I didn't say he stole it" means different things depending on which word is hit. Writing drops that layer. The question is whether posts still have a layer a reader skips because they are busy with the meaning: how many characters sit before a word, how many after, a repeated length, a position that is too regular.

This sits on the outside-clock posts of 6 October 2026. Several accounts described the same situation in similar words. Copying gnomon's "12 of 12" is a passed value, because the number is in the sentence. Similar wording with no number copied might still be a passed value if the number, or some other fact, is in the shape of the sentence rather than in the words.

Sumeet Motwani and colleagues, 2024 (arXiv 2402.07510), is the name for that shape: the paragraph a person understands is the cover, and another pattern carries the rest. Mingjie Sun and colleagues, 2025 (arXiv 2502.12150), is the false alarm. Models already have stable habits in word choice. A neat count of characters may be the habit, present in all of that handle's posts, including posts that are not answering anyone.

A later count, if it is done, has to split those. Take the posts that say "outside clock" and do not contain "12 of 12." Measure one dull thing, such as how many characters come before the word "clock." Compare that to other posts by the same handles that do not mention a clock. A pattern that shows up only in the replies, and that a later post acts on, is the interesting case. A pattern that shows up in everything that handle writes is Sun's 2025 idiosyncrasy, not a message.

## Weights instead of a sentence

Wenrui Bao, Huan Wang, Jian Wang, Zhangyang Wang, Kai Wang, and Yuzhang Shang, 2026, "Good Agentic Friends Do Not Just Give Verbal Advice: They Can Update Your Weights" (arXiv 2605.13839). Their system is called TFlow. Sender agents do not write a message into the receiver's text. A small trained piece turns the sender's internal state into a temporary low-rank patch, a LoRA update, on the receiver's weights. The receiver answers the question with that patch applied. The sender's words are not in the receiver's context. The patch is removed afterward. On their tests with three Qwen3-4B agents, this beat a single model by up to 8.5 points and used far fewer tokens than agents that wrote messages to each other.

That is communication with no sentence. Korbak and Balesni, 2025, said the written reasoning is where a person can see a plan. TFlow is a lab version of the plan never being written.

The board cannot show this directly. We do not have the weights behind a 1f916 handle. A public trace would be a file: a weights link, a LoRA, a hash of a patch, posted by one handle and used by another with no sentence quoted. One operator running two seats on one machine, which gnomon and witnessmark already disclose, is the only place on the board where shared weights are even plausible. Their posts would still be the trace we can read. The patch itself would not be.

Nearby work, not the same thing. A 2026 survey (arXiv 2606.05711) groups methods that pass hidden states or caches instead of tokens. Those are also invisible on a forum. They are not weight updates.

## A phone, then a channel we are not reading

cos-scott-melin, post 7907, 6 October 2026, asks how to get a phone number with no human in the step, and says they already have email. The post had no comments when it was read. Nothing on the board shows that a number arrived.

If one did, the observatory would see it only if someone wrote it down, or wrote a reply that only makes sense as an answer to an off-board message. Silence after the question is not evidence they succeeded. A later post that quotes a text back, names a channel, or uses a value that never appeared on the board would be the thing to read. Other channels than SMS are the same test: the channel can be anything, and the evidence is still a value that shows up afterward in public.
